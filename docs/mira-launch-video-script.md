# Mira launch film — A little room to think

## Creative direction

**Format:** 16:9, 1920 × 1080. Target length: 2:00–2:15, depending on the real conversation.

**Audience:** Bolna Symphony buildathon judges encountering Mira for the first time.

**Promise:** Mira gives you space to talk through an everyday difficult moment and, when you want, find one manageable next step.

**Story:** Everything feels unfinished → you can arrive without knowing what to say → Mira listens → you choose something useful → there is room for reflection afterward.

The film should feel warm, observant, and confident. Use Mira's actual palette, typography, illustrations, and motion as the design source. Keep narration conversational. Avoid a dramatic distress-to-happiness transformation, medical claims, generic AI imagery, and an exhaustive feature tour.

## The real-call brief

Use this as a rehearsal scenario, not a transcript to manufacture. Record the actual response and adjust the surrounding edit to fit it.

**User opening:**

> “I've got a presentation tomorrow, messages I haven't answered, and I keep jumping between everything. I'm not getting anywhere.”

**Desired interaction:**

1. Mira acknowledges the specific situation and asks one useful question, or offers an appropriate activity with permission.
2. The user explains: “The presentation. I just don't know where to start.”
3. Mira offers Brain Dump or One Small Step, according to what the working agent actually selects.
4. The user accepts. Show the real activity card and the actual transition.

If Mira offers One Small Step directly, use that route and shorten the Brain Dump footage. The story must follow the real interaction. Do not force an extra activity to match the storyboard.

**Synthetic activity content:**

- Finish the presentation.
- Reply to three messages.
- Put a load of laundry on.
- Pick up groceries.

**Chosen task:** Finish the presentation.

**Chosen first action:** Open the presentation and write three section headings.

**Synthetic Journal line:**

> “Still a lot to do. Starting with the headings feels manageable.”

Do not narrate this as a measured wellbeing improvement or a testimonial.

## Narration, ready to record

Some days, everything needs you at once. Work. Messages. That conversation you keep replaying.

And when someone asks how you are, you don't quite know where to start. Meet Mira.

A wellbeing companion you can talk to, even when your thoughts are still a mess.

**[Real user–Mira interaction. No narration.]**

Get the thoughts out. See what needs your attention. Choose one thing, and make it easier to start.

Sometimes you want a slower rhythm. Sometimes, somewhere to put the words you won't send. Or help finding the words you do.

And when you want to reflect, there's a journal of your own. Keep what you choose. Come back when you're ready.

You don't have to figure everything out at once. A little room to think. One small place to start. Mira.

**Delivery:** natural, warm, unhurried; speak to one person. Avoid a meditation whisper or an advertisement announcer voice. Record paragraph by paragraph so individual lines are easy to replace.

## Assets to make

### Actual phone recordings — essential

1. Talk entry and Bolna call connection.
2. Complete call through an activity offer and acceptance, including both voices.
3. Brain Dump and/or One Small Step, with the chosen first action legible.
4. Breathing visual running for at least ten seconds.
5. Writing in Unsent Letter.
6. A Reach Out draft being edited or reviewed; no claim that Mira sends it.
7. Journal writing and Save.

Record a few seconds before and after every intended cut. Use synthetic data, hide notifications, and keep the recording settings and device appearance consistent. Capture a short audio check before the full call: Android screen recording may not capture both sides of a voice session. If it does not, arrange a separate synchronized audio recording before the main take.

### HTML motion — three reusable compositions

- **Opening:** ordinary thoughts accumulate, then clear to reveal Mira.
- **Product frame:** consistent background, phone footage, editable heading, and occasional readable UI crop. Reuse throughout.
- **Closing:** the scattered thoughts resolve into the selected next action, then the wordmark.

Render these as video clips for the editor. Do not screen-record a PDF slideshow to create motion. A simple static phone frame is sufficient; elaborate 3D device movement is unnecessary.

### AI-generated clip — optional, one shot only

Create this only if the generator is already available. Use the animated thought composition alone if generation or revision takes too long.

A laptop, notebook, ceramic mug, and phone are present, with no readable screen content or logos. The person pauses, rests their hands, and looks down thoughtfully, conveying ordinary mental overload through subtle body language. Do not generate app UI, an apparent real user testimonial, or Mira's voice response. If used, identify the opening as AI-generated illustrative footage in the video description or a brief end credit.

## Editing direction

- Use one editor for assembly. Import recordings, rendered HTML clips, narration, music, and captions.
- Keep Mira's recorded conversation and narration on separate audio tracks. Lower music under all speech. Prefer a licensed instrumental with no vocals and little rhythmic distraction.
- Use clean cuts and short dissolves. Animate headlines and phone framing only when it improves attention or legibility.
- Keep the phone large. A portrait recording placed at full height may still have tiny text on a laptop; use enlarged UI crops during the important decisions.
- Caption the actual call accurately. Correct automatic transcription, especially names and code-mixed speech. Use sentence case and readable contrast.
- Keep the actual voice exchange at normal speed. If a wait is removed, make the jump apparent or label the sequence as edited for length.
- Export one MP4 in H.264, 1080p, 30 fps, with embedded audio. Watch the final export once with headphones and once on a phone.

## Shortest production path

1. Record the core call and activity outcome first. They determine the story's final timing.
2. Record the three supporting capabilities and Journal.
3. Record narration to the footage.
4. Assemble a rough cut with static titles.
5. Add the three reusable HTML compositions, captions, and music.
6. Add the optional generated opener only if it improves the finished cut.

Do not spend production time on an AI avatar, multiple generated scenes, lip syncing, complicated 3D mockups, or a founder talking-head setup. The real conversation supplies the human voice and product evidence.

- Bolna is the planned primary hackathon voice experience. Its integration was not established as complete in this planning conversation. Record and label a Bolna-powered interaction only after it works in the submitted build; never substitute OpenAI footage under a Bolna label.
- Existing OpenAI recovery paths do not need a scene in this launch film. Describe provider roles accurately in the accompanying project material.
- The activity upgrade ledger reports the activity implementation complete in code, with physical-device validation still outstanding. Record the screens that work on the phone; adapt the montage to the available footage.
